Market research

What we learned from five food businesses.

We studied three South African chicken brands and two food-delivery apps before building Kasi-Crunch.

Chicken Licken · SA fried-chicken chain (Chicken Licken n.d.)

Does well

  • Strong, local, humorous brand
  • Affordable meals and combos

Could improve

  • Stores are not on every kasi street
  • Large chain, not community-owned

Nando's · Flame-grilled peri-peri chicken (Nando's n.d.)

Does well

  • Clear spice levels on the menu
  • Recognisable brand and online ordering

Could improve

  • Pricier for students
  • Mostly in malls and suburbs

Galito's · Flame-grilled chicken chain (Galito's n.d.)

Does well

  • Simple menu with clear combos
  • Easy-to-read website

Could improve

  • Fewer outlets in townships
  • Less known outside the cities

Mr D · Food delivery app (Mr D n.d.)

Does well

  • Fast mobile ordering and live tracking
  • Fees shown before checkout

Could improve

  • Coverage concentrated in urban areas
  • Fees add up on small orders

Uber Eats · Food delivery app (Uber Eats n.d.)

Does well

  • Huge restaurant choice
  • Regular deals and promotions in the app

Could improve

  • Service and delivery fees add up
  • Limited reach in some townships

The gap Kasi-Crunch fills

The big brands make food people love, but mostly sell from malls and suburbs. Delivery apps make ordering easy, but their coverage and fees don't always suit township customers. Kasi-Crunch is our own kasi kitchen with simple online ordering, fair distance-based delivery, free collection, and a Crunch Pass priced by how far you live.

How the research shaped our prototype

  • Spicy items clearly tagged, like Nando's spice levels
  • Simple meal combos, like Galito's and Chicken Licken
  • The exact delivery fee shown before checkout, like Mr D
  • A delivery subscription priced by distance, so nearby customers pay less
  • Free collection and pay-on-arrival for customers without bank cards
