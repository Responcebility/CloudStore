🔬 Market research

What we learned from five digital businesses

Before building CloudStore we studied five real digital businesses that sell, deliver or take payments online. For each one we noted what it does well, what it does badly, and the gap CloudStore can fill.

📦

Takealot

South African online retailer and marketplace (Takealot n.d.)

A large South African online store that also lets third-party sellers list products and delivers nationwide.

👍 Does well

  • Huge product range
  • Trusted brand and nationwide delivery
  • Clear order tracking

👎 Does badly

  • Seller onboarding suits established, registered businesses
  • Little room for informal township vendors
🛵

Mr D

Food and grocery delivery app (Mr D n.d.)

An on-demand delivery app for restaurant meals and groceries with live order tracking.

👍 Does well

  • Fast, simple ordering on mobile
  • Live tracking of deliveries
  • Clear fees before checkout

👎 Does badly

  • Built around restaurants and chain stores
  • Coverage is concentrated in urban and suburban areas
💳

Yoco

Payments for small businesses (Yoco n.d.)

Card machines and online payment tools that help small South African businesses accept card payments.

👍 Does well

  • Low barrier for small merchants
  • Simple tools aimed at entrepreneurs

👎 Does badly

  • Helps vendors get paid, but not get found
  • No shared marketplace for customers to browse
🏷️

Facebook Marketplace

Free local listings (Meta n.d.)

A free place for people and small sellers to list items for buyers nearby.

👍 Does well

  • Free to list
  • Very large local audience

👎 Does badly

  • Deals happen in chats, with no structured checkout or delivery
  • Trust and scam risks
🧶

Etsy

Global marketplace for handmade goods (Etsy n.d.)

An international marketplace where makers open shops to sell handmade and vintage items.

👍 Does well

  • Shop pages that tell the maker's story
  • Strong focus on handmade crafts

👎 Does badly

  • Listing and transaction fees add up for micro sellers
  • International focus and shipping costs for local crafters
☁

The gap CloudStore fills

Our problem statement, based on these cases

Facebook Marketplace shows that local buyers and sellers want to trade online, but its chat-based deals offer no checkout, no delivery and little trust. Takealot and Mr D offer smooth checkout and delivery, but are built for registered businesses and restaurants. CloudStore combines the two: a structured shop, clear fees and local delivery, made for informal community vendors.

How the research shaped our prototype

🧾

Fees up front

Like Mr D, the cart shows the delivery fee and total before checkout, with no surprises.

🏪

Vendor stories

Like Etsy, every product shows the vendor and their community, building trust and pride.

💸

Free to start

Unlike Etsy's fees, the Starter plan is free; vendors pay commission only on sales.

✅

Structured checkout

Unlike chat-based deals on Facebook Marketplace, orders, totals and status are recorded.

💵

Pay on delivery

Cash or card on delivery suits shoppers without bank cards; a payment gateway like Yoco is a planned integration.

📍

Local first

Focus on townships and campuses that larger platforms serve less.
